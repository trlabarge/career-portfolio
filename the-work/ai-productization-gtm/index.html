AI Productization and Go-To-Market

CEI could deliver AI work and had nothing a client could actually buy. Marketing built the offering, named it CEI Clairvoyance, and took it to market across brand, web, and content. It generated more than $1MM in AI project pipeline.

The situation

The capability was real. The offering did not exist yet.

In 2023 CEI shifted its global go-to-market to prioritize AI-led offerings. Delivery was ready for that. CEI had the engineers, the enterprise platform experience, and the client relationships to put production AI systems in place.

The market-facing side had nothing to point at. There was no name for the practice, no description of what an engagement included, no answer to how long it took or what a client had to commit, and no page on the website for a prospect researching AI to land on. Every competitor in IT services added the word AI to their homepage that same year, which made being capable and quiet the worst available position.

The gap was productization. A capability had to become an offering with a name, a shape, a first step, and a story behind it before any campaign could carry it.

The work

Four moves, in the order they had to happen.

Naming came before messaging, messaging came before the website, and the website came before the content program. Each one was unusable until the one before it existed.

01

Create the offering and the brand behind it

Marketing built the AI Innovation Practice the way you build a product. That meant a name, an identity, a positioning statement, and the sales and marketing materials that let a distributed team sell the same thing the same way.

The name landed on CEI Clairvoyance. It carries foresight and pattern recognition, which is what the offering delivers, and it reads as a practice a client engages rather than software they license. The identity keeps the CEI mark locked to the new name, so the practice inherited CEI’s existing credibility instead of starting a second brand from zero. The letters A and I sit highlighted inside the wordmark, which does the positioning work before anyone reads a line of copy.

02

Answer the questions every AI buyer was actually asking

Interest in AI ran high and buying confidence ran low. Prospects wanted to know what CEI’s AI offering was, how it would work, how long it would take to implement, and how they could engage without signing up for a transformation program.

So the offering went to market as a staged path with real durations attached to it. A short workshop to find the use cases, a fixed engagement to prove value on one of them, then ongoing adoption. A buyer could see exactly where they would start and what that first step cost them in time.

  1. Step 12 days

    AI Innovation Workshop Build an understanding of how AI tools can be incorporated, and identify specific use cases for the organization.

  2. Step 26 to 8 weeks

    Proof of Value Engagement Implement one use case into production, measure the results, and determine the ROI impact.

  3. Step 3Steady state

    CEI Clairvoyance Incrementally adopt AI tools, models, and automation, maturing toward fully AI-powered operations.

The engagement path as it was published. Two days to a decision, six to eight weeks to a measured result.

03

Rebuild the website around the new practice

Website creative, copy, and pages were reimagined so AI read as one of CEI’s core service areas rather than a capability buried in a services list. The homepage gave the practice prime real estate above the fold, with Unlock the Power of AI as the primary path in, and AI Innovation became a top-level navigation item in its own right.

Seven new pages went live underneath it, one for each distinct AI use case and application. Clairvoyance Foundation, Enterprise Assistants, AI Knowledge Management, AI Managed Services, GitHub Copilot, Clairvoyance Automation, and Clairvoyance Data Analytics. A prospect searching for a specific problem now found a page about that problem instead of a general AI overview, and the content and campaign programs finally had somewhere to send traffic that converted.

04

Publish enough to be credible in search and in the room

A content marketing plan put 25 articles into market over three months, aimed at three outcomes at once. Establish CEI as an AI thought leader, show business leaders the specific functions and tasks where AI could be applied, and rank the site for AI keywords it had never competed for.

The subject matter stayed close to the buyer’s job rather than the technology. Articles covered AI in the workplace, AI-powered managed services, coding assistance, cybersecurity, computer vision, and the generative versus predictive AI question that surfaced in nearly every early client conversation. That volume in that window is what moved 73 AI keywords onto Google, and the seven use case pages are what those articles pointed at.

Recruiting got easier alongside the pipeline. Engineers evaluating CEI as an employer could see the AI practice, read the work, and understand what they would be building, which mattered in a year when every services firm claimed the same capability and few could show it. CEI Clairvoyance is still the AI practice CEI takes to market today.

What ran alongside it

The AI keywords were one pillar of a larger search program.

Those 73 AI keywords landed inside a broader SEO and content engine running across all five of CEI’s service pillars over the same period. That program took the site from 463 to 810 ranking keywords and grew organic sessions 47 percent year over year.

It is written up separately in SEO and Content Marketing Growth, which covers the ICP-led content model and the technical work underneath it.
