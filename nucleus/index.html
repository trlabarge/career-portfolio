ABX plan

Nucleus Security: A Starting ABX Hypothesis

A signal-led ABX plan for 200 target accounts. It finds the accounts in a buying cycle, reaches the practitioners who drive wins, and stays with deals through close.

Assumptions are marked throughout and should be validated against Nucleus data.

The plan in 30 seconds

  1. Rank the 200 accounts by buying signals as well as ICP fit.
  2. Reach the practitioners who drive wins.
  3. Support deals after the meeting is booked.
  4. Point events at accounts already in a buying cycle.
  5. Shift budget toward decision-stage support.

Current state

The foundation is in place, with several gaps worth testing.

  • 200target accounts, chosen on ICP fit and tech stack signals
  • 3-4xearly pipeline read against the control group, since July
  • 6-9month commercial sales cycle
  • 12-24month government sales cycle
  • 3major airline customers, plus an Aviation ISAC presence

Strengths

  • A fit-based target list and a control group
  • OnFire, G2, ZoomInfo and HubSpot in place
  • BDRs already acting on intent signals
  • Events that book a high volume of meetings
  • Sales support for marketing staying involved in deals

Gaps

  • Knowing which accounts are in a buying cycle
  • Marketing makes little use of intent signals
  • Paid is display and retargeting only, with no full journey or vertical versions
  • Little nurture after a meeting is booked
  • Event meetings convert to sales less often than other channels

Other context. Content syndication has produced low-converting leads. The G2 renewal is uncertain. Channel is 100% partner-led, and channel marketing is early-stage.

Priorities

Top 3 Priorities to Close Current Gaps

  1. 01

    Find the accounts in a buying cycle

    Combine the intent sources into one view of which accounts are active now. Put more budget and BDR time there.

  2. 02

    Reach the practitioners who drive wins

    Early Gong analysis links more director-level and below attendees on early calls to more wins. Aim early content, ads and outreach at them.

  3. 03

    Stay in the deal after the meeting

    With 6-9 month cycles, content built with AEs keeps deals moving between the first meeting and close.

A common rule of thumb is that 5-10% of a TAM is in a buying cycle at any time. If that is roughly true for Nucleus's 200 target accounts, timing matters just as much as ICP fit.

Events

Event meetings should turn into revenue as often as meetings from other channels.

  1. Pre-target

    Book meetings with Tier 1 and 2 accounts already showing buying signals.

  2. Follow up in 48 hours

    Practitioner content tied to what each account cares about.

  3. Nurture to close

    Event opportunities get the same AE-built nurture as every other deal. Win rates are compared by channel after each event.

Open questions

Five questions to answer in the first month.

How many of the 200 accounts show usable intent today?

This sets how large Tier 1 can be and whether there are enough signals to tier on.

Which signals came before past pipeline?

Looking back at closed deals is the fastest way to set thresholds.

At which stage do event deals fall behind other channels?

A drop before the opportunity and a drop after it call for different fixes.

What does the Gong analysis show by deal type?

The practitioner pattern may differ for government or larger deals.

Who approves content used in active deals?

Post-meeting nurture only works if sales trusts it.
