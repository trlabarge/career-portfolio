Nucleus Security: A Starting ABX Hypothesis

Built from our two conversations, and meant to be pressure-tested against Nucleus data.

You know this program far better than I do. This is how I would start and what I would want to test, written from the outside with a partial view. Some of it will be wrong in ways only the data can show.

01

What I heard

A short summary of what you told me. Please correct anything I got wrong.

  • The list

    A 200-account ABX list built on ICP fit and tech stack signals, such as multiple scanners and a high integration count.

  • Early results

    ABX launched in July against a control group. The early read is 3-4x pipeline versus control. Commercial cycles of 6-9 months, 12-24 months for government, and large deals make that hard to validate yet.

  • Intent data

    OnFire, G2, ZoomInfo (Web Insights and intent) and HubSpot are in place. The G2 renewal is uncertain at year end. BDRs use the signals for outbound. Marketing uses them less.

  • Paid today

    Display prospecting and retargeting on Google and LinkedIn, with pain-point and "better together" integration messaging. It is not yet a full awareness-to-conversion journey, and it is not verticalized.

  • The key gap

    Knowing which accounts are actually in a buying cycle. Making ABX more targeted without shrinking audiences too far to reach efficiently.

  • Events

    Events produce lots of meetings. Held rates are lower than for web signups, and event opportunities convert less downstream.

  • Who is on the calls

    You are comparing Gong call attendees with Salesforce opportunity contacts. The early finding is that more practitioners, director level and below, on early calls correlates with winning.

  • After the meeting

    Mid-to-bottom funnel nurture after a meeting is booked is largely absent. Sales is open to marketing staying involved after an opportunity opens, as long as it supports the deal narrative.

  • Content syndication

    Tried repeatedly. It produces low-converting leads.

  • Aviation

    A vertical opportunity, with three major airline customers and an Aviation ISAC presence.

  • Channel

    Channel is 100% partner-led. Channel marketing is early-stage and mostly owned by the channel team.

02

Where I'd focus

Three priorities, each tied to something you described as a gap.

  1. 1

    Find the accounts in a buying cycle

    The list selects for fit. I would add timing by combining the intent sources you have into one view of which accounts are active now, and point more budget and BDR time at them.

    A common rule of thumb, and not Nucleus data, is that only 5-10% of a market is in-market at any given time. If that is even roughly true for the 200, most of the list is not buying right now, which makes spotting the accounts that are worth extra effort.

  2. 2

    Reach the practitioners who seem to drive wins

    If your Gong finding holds up, the people to reach early are directors and the practitioners on their teams. I would aim more content, ads and BDR outreach at them and treat their engagement as a signal in its own right.

  3. 3

    Keep marketing involved after the meeting

    With 6-9 month cycles, there is a long stretch after a meeting is booked where marketing does little today. Content built with AEs could help hold meetings and keep deals moving, as long as it supports the deal narrative.

03

Account tiering

Three tiers inside the 200, so effort follows signals. The tier definitions and plays are starting hypotheses.

A small, high-touch group

Fit accounts showing buying signals, such as intent spikes, web visits, engagement and practitioner activity.

Hypothesis Concentrating effort on these accounts produces more meetings held per account than spreading the same effort across the list.

What marketing does

  • Coordinated outreach with BDRs, timed to the signal
  • Practitioner-focused content
  • Tighter ad targeting
  • Account-specific plays built with the AE

Fit accounts with some engagement

Accounts that fit and have engaged a little, without a clear buying signal yet.

Hypothesis A steady, lower-cost presence keeps these accounts warm, so a signal turns into a meeting faster when it shows up.

What marketing does

  • Lighter-touch ads
  • Nurture by role and pain point
  • Watched for signals that would move them to Tier 1

The rest of the list

Every other account on the 200.

Hypothesis Keeping this tier broad protects audience size on LinkedIn and display, so tighter targeting in Tier 1 does not shrink reach for the whole program.

What marketing does

  • Broad enough LinkedIn and display reach to stay efficient
  • Pain-point and "better together" integration messaging
  • Signal monitoring, so accounts can move up

Accounts move between tiers every month

As signals change, accounts move up or down. Tier 1 stays small enough that BDRs and AEs can give each account real attention. I would set the thresholds with the BDR lead.

Signals I'd test

  • Intent spikesRising research activity on relevant topics, from OnFire, G2 and ZoomInfo.
  • Web visitsRepeat visits or visits to high-intent pages, from ZoomInfo Web Insights and HubSpot.
  • Engagement breadthSeveral people at one account engaging with ads, email or content.
  • Practitioner activityDirector-level and below contacts engaging, given the early Gong finding.
  • Stack changesChanges in the tech stack signals the list was built on.
  • Competitor connections Idea to testPractitioners at a target account connecting with competitors' sales reps on LinkedIn. I have not tested this. It is worth checking whether it can be observed reliably and whether it comes before buying activity.

04

The account journey

What marketing does at each stage. The emphasis is on the stretch after a meeting is booked.

  1. 1

    Aware

    Broad LinkedIn and display reach to the list, with pain-point and integration messaging.

  2. 2

    Engaged

    Role-based content and retargeting. Signals go to BDRs with context on what the account engaged with.

  3. 3

    Meeting

    Confirm within 48 hours with practitioner content related to what the account showed interest in.

  4. 4

    Opportunity

    Nurture built with AEs and aimed at the practitioners on the deal. Ads to the buying group.

  5. 5

    Closed

    Wins feed stories and references back into earlier stages. Wins and losses both get a look at which signals came first.

Where I would put the most new effort

Post-meeting nurture, built with AEs

Aimed at the practitioners on each deal. Sales signs off on it so it supports the deal narrative.

  • Competitive

    Material that helps practitioners compare options during evaluation.

  • Pain-point

    Content on the problems the account raised in early calls.

  • Vertical

    Industry-specific proof where it exists, starting with aviation.

05

Events inside ABX

Bring events into the ABX program to raise held rates and downstream conversion.

  1. Before the event

    Pre-target Tier 1 and 2 accounts

    Ads and BDR outreach to fit accounts before the event, to book meetings with the right people.

  2. Within 48 hours

    Confirm every meeting

    A confirmation with practitioner content tied to what the account cares about.

  3. After the event

    Compare against prior events

    Held rates and opportunity rates side by side with earlier events, to see whether either one moved.

Hypothesis Meetings booked with accounts that were already showing signals hold and convert at higher rates than meetings booked with no prior signal.

06

Aviation pilot

Three major airline customers, the aviation use case, and an Aviation ISAC presence make aviation a reasonable place to test a verticalized version of the program on a small set of accounts.

  • Pick a small set of aviation accounts from the list or close to it
  • Build content on the aviation use case, using customer proof as far as references allow
  • Use the Aviation ISAC presence for reach and credibility
  • Compare engagement and meetings held against similar accounts without vertical content

What it would tell us Whether a vertical version earns its extra content cost, and whether the same approach should extend to other verticals.

07

How we'd know it's working

Pipeline from these accounts will take quarters to show up. These leading indicators should move first.

  • Engagement depth

    How many people at each account engage, and how often.

  • Practitioners engaged per account

    Director-level and below contacts engaging, tracked by tier.

  • Meetings held

    Held as a share of booked, by source, with events tracked on their own.

  • Stage velocity

    Time from meeting to opportunity and between opportunity stages.

Keep the control group

Every change gets measured against the control group already in place, so results stay comparable as the program changes.

08

First 90 days

Learn first, then pick a small number of tests, then run them on a regular rhythm.

Learn

Check these hypotheses against the data and the people who run the program.

  • Go through the data with you, including the 200-account list, the intent sources, the control group and past pipeline
  • Learn from you how the program has evolved since July
  • Meet the team
  • Spend time with sales to understand how deals move
  • Spend time with BDRs on how they use signals today

Pick the first tests

Choose a small number of tests based on what Month 1 shows.

  • Pick the first tests and agree how each will be measured against the control group
  • Set signal thresholds for each tier with the BDR lead
  • Plan the first post-meeting content with AEs

Run and review

Get the first tests running and set the rhythm for reviewing them.

  • Launch the first tests
  • Run the first monthly tier review
  • Set a review rhythm with you, sales and the BDR team
  • Report on the leading indicators

09

Questions I'd want answered first

  1. How many of the 200 accounts show usable intent today?
  2. Which signals came before past pipeline?
  3. Where is the biggest drop in the event funnel?
  4. What does the Gong analysis show by deal type?
  5. Who approves content used in active deals?
