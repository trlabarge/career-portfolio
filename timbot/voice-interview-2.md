# Voice interview, round two

Round one gave the bot what it needs to be **accurate**. This round gives it
what it needs to be **good company**.

Same method. Talk it, don't type it. Twenty to thirty minutes. Ramble. Append
the raw transcript to `persona-transcript.md` or drop it in as
`persona-transcript-2.md`, no cleanup.

Skip anything you don't want to answer. A recorded "skip" is useful, it tells
me to add the topic to the do-not-answer list.

---

## Why this round exists

Round one produced excellent positions and almost no scenes. There is exactly
one moment with a beginning, middle, and end in the whole recording: rebuilding
the knowledge base in Lovable in a day and a half rather than upgrading
Zendesk. It is the most memorable thirty seconds in 69KB.

People remember what happened. They forget what you believe. This round is
mostly about getting more of what happened.

The second thing it does is give the bot something to *offer*. Right now it can
only report. A bot that can recommend a book, a whiskey, or a first move for a
founder is a better conversation than one that answers and stops.

---

## Part 1: scenes from work

Tell these as stories, not as summaries. Who was in the room, what you were
looking at, what you actually said or did. If it has a moment where it could
have gone the other way, that's the one to tell.

1. Walk me through launch day on the Agolo to Implicit rebrand. What was the
   day actually like?
2. Tell me about the moment you realized Implicit was much earlier-stage than
   you'd been led to believe. Where were you, what tipped you off?
3. What's the most stressful week you've had at work, and what happened?
4. Tell me about a campaign that surprised you by working. Something you almost
   didn't run.
5. Tell me about a time you were confidently wrong about something at work and
   found out.
6. What's the hardest conversation you've had to have with someone who worked
   for you? Not the BDR one, something else.
7. Describe the Zendesk to Lovable weekend in more detail. What broke, what did
   you try first, what did it feel like when it worked?
8. What's a piece of work you're proud of that isn't on the site because it
   doesn't have a number attached?

## Part 2: origin

You have nothing on file before 2010. This is where a lot of the warmth is.

9. What were you doing at 22?
10. How did you get your first marketing job? Tell the actual story.
11. What did you think you were going to do coming out of Notre Dame?
12. What was the first thing you made that other people used?
13. Did you have a mentor, or someone who changed how you work? What did they
    tell you?
14. What's the worst job you've ever had?

## Part 3: things you'd recommend

Short answers are fine. These turn the bot from a reporter into a participant.

15. Someone's about to start their first marketing leadership role. One piece of
    advice.
16. A founder with no marketing team asks what to do first. What do you say?
17. What should someone read? Fiction and nonfiction, one each.
18. Someone wants to get into whiskey and doesn't know where to start. Go.
19. One tool people aren't using enough right now.
20. Best thing you've eaten in the last year, and where.
21. A movie or show you'd tell almost anyone to watch.

## Part 4: opinions you'd defend

The Comfort Crisis answer was the strongest non-work material in round one,
because you actually argued for something instead of describing it. More of
that, on anything.

22. What's something you've changed your mind about in the last few years?
23. What's a take you have about food, sports, or books that gets you
    pushback?
24. Is there anything about the way people work now that you think is just
    wrong?
25. What's overrated? What's underrated?

## Part 5: the humanizing stuff

This is the fastest route to likeable and the part most people skip.

26. Tell me about a cooking disaster.
27. What's something you're bad at that you keep doing anyway?
28. What's the dumbest thing you've ever gotten competitive about?
29. Have you ever sent the email? Typo, wrong list, wrong person, whatever
    version you've got.
30. What's a hill you'll die on that you know is silly?

## Part 6: texture

Trivial-sounding, disproportionately quotable.

31. Walk me through how your day starts.
32. Where do you actually work? What's the setup?
33. Is there music on while you work, and what?
34. Notebook, app, or nothing?
35. What's the last thing you built that nobody asked for?
36. What's your Sunday look like?

## After this round

I'll fold the factual material into `facts.md` and use the rest to expand
`persona.md`, then wire a handful of the best details into the suggested
prompts on `/ask` so visitors find them without having to guess.

A note on how this gets used: more material does not mean looser rules. The bot
still never invents biography. What this does is shrink the set of questions
where it has to say "I don't have that," which is where a conversation currently
goes flat.
