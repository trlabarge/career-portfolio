Part 1: RSAC Conference 2027

April 5 to 8, 2027 · Moscone Center, San Francisco

Goal: $200K in pipeline from $25K (travel not included)

How I'd approach it

I'd skip the show floor. A booth at RSAC would use up most of the budget on space, so I'd put the money into meetings booked before the event and a couple of experiences people remember and talk about afterward.

The math, working backward

  • 10opportunities ($200K ÷ $20K average deal)
  • 25meetings (at a 40% meeting-to-opportunity rate)
  • ~17booked before the event through outreach
  • ~8from two hosted dinners
  • $2,500cost per opportunity

Who we'd go after

  • Security leaders (CISOs and SecOps and AppSec directors) at companies with growing security teams, which overlaps with Part 2 so one list does double duty
  • Labs users and existing customers at target accounts, since they already know us
  • Talent acquisition leaders, if the hiring assessment is live by April
  • A target list of about 340 accounts that are attending or based in the Bay Area

Bold idea

"The 20-Minute Rule" street challenge

A coffee cart or pop-up a block from Moscone, off the show floor. Anyone who solves a 20-minute challenge gets free coffee, and a live leaderboard ranks companies against each other. Every company on the board is a lead, the board gives people something to post about, and it's SkillBit's whole idea (prove the skill) in a format people walk up to. I'd check RSAC's rules and city permits first.

Getting meetings on the calendar

  1. Early February (8 weeks out)Build the list and start BDR outreach
  2. Late FebruaryDinner invites go out with a personal note from Thomas
  3. The pitch"Let's run your team through a 20-minute challenge while you're in town"
  4. Week beforeConfirm every meeting and send calendar holds with the location
  5. At the eventThe AE and Thomas take meetings, and the BDRs book follow-ups on site
  6. Within 48 hoursPersonal follow-up that includes the person's challenge results

Where AI helps

  • AI

    A target list from public signals. The same workflow I built at Implicit (which drove about 30% of new leads) can pull the RSAC speaker and sponsor lists and LinkedIn posts like "headed to RSAC." Claude scores the accounts and drafts outreach that mentions their talk or post.

  • AI

    Personalized challenge invites. Claude reads a prospect's open job postings and picks the matching Labs challenge, so the invite says "here's the challenge we'd give your cloud security candidates, try it with us at RSAC."

  • AI

    An instant results email. Anyone who plays the street challenge gets a short skills snapshot within the hour, with a link to book time.

  • AI

    Same-day meeting follow-ups. Notes from each meeting become a draft follow-up the AE approves that night.

Where we can be scrappy

  • Scrappy

    Co-host the dinners with a vendor that sells to the same buyer without competing with us (identity or SOC tooling, for example), splitting the cost and the guest list. That turns two dinners into four, or frees up $5K.

  • Scrappy

    Ask customers to host. A security leader from a customer co-hosting a dinner fills seats better than a vendor invite does.

  • Scrappy

    Use community events. Sponsor a challenge at BSides SF or a local ISSA meetup that week instead of throwing our own event.

  • Scrappy

    Publish what we learned. A week after the show, share anonymized leaderboard results ("What we learned from 300 practitioners in 20 minutes"). It's a second round of follow-up and a PR story from data we already collected.

Part 2: Growing security teams as a buying signal

Goal: 15 opportunities ($300K in pipeline) next quarter from two BDRs

The math

  • 30meetings (15 opportunities at a 50% meeting-to-opportunity rate)
  • 1,300accounts of capacity (2 BDRs × 50 accounts a week × 13 weeks)
  • 1,000accounts needed at a 3% meeting rate
  • 77%of capacity, which leaves room for a slow start

The capacity is there, but only if the signal lifts the reply rate above cold outbound. At 2% we'd need 1,500 accounts, which is more than two BDRs can work.

Spotting the companies

  • Tier A

    Three or more open security roles in the last 60 days, plus a Labs user at the company or a new security leader

  • Tier B

    Three or more open security roles

  • Tier C

    Security headcount growth on LinkedIn, a recent funding round, or a compliance trigger like SOC 2 or FedRAMP

Sources: job boards and career pages pulled through Clay or something similar, Sales Navigator headcount growth by department, and Labs signups matched to company domains.

When to reach out

Two moments for every account:

  1. 1While they're hiring (within one to two weeks of the posting)

    The hiring assessment, sent to the hiring manager with talent acquisition copied

  2. 230 to 60 days after the hires start

    Onboarding and upskilling for the new team, which gives every account a second touch at no extra sourcing cost

What we'd say

Four touches over two weeks across email, LinkedIn and phone, and every message names their actual open roles.

Bold idea

A free "job description to skills test" tool

A security leader pastes in a job description, and Claude returns a 20-minute assessment plan for that role built from SkillBit's library. Anyone who uses it is showing us the exact signal we're looking for, so outbound starts generating inbound. It's cheap to build as a simple AI-powered page, and the timing lines up with the hiring assessment launch.

Where AI helps

  • AI

    Read the postings. Claude pulls the skills each posting asks for (cloud, incident response, AppSec) and maps them to SkillBit labs, so each message says exactly what the company is hiring for.

  • AI

    Draft the first message. Claude writes the email from the posting, and the BDR edits and sends it. That cuts research time and keeps 50 accounts a week realistic.

  • AI

    An automatic weekly scorecard. Every Monday a Slack post compares results to plan, so nobody builds a report by hand.

  • AI

    Tag every reply. AI sorts reply text by objection ("already hired," "no budget," "wrong person") so we can fix the message every week.

With two BDRs, I'd use AI to take the research and first drafts so their time goes to conversations.

Where we can be scrappy

  • Scrappy

    Track job changes for Labs users. A Labs user who joins a new company is a warm contact, often on a team that's growing.

  • Scrappy

    Watch for "I'm hiring" posts. Security leaders post about open roles on LinkedIn, sometimes before the job boards, and the listening workflow catches them quickly.

  • Scrappy

    Partner with security recruiters. Staffing agencies see hiring before it's posted, and a referral fee or co-marketing deal costs very little.

  • Scrappy

    Make an easy offer. "Free assessments for your first five candidates on this role" is an easier yes than a demo.

  • Scrappy

    Send a short video to Tier A accounts. A 60-second screen recording of their job posting next to the matching challenge takes a BDR about five minutes.
