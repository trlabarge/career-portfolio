Featured initiative

“The 3-Minute Flag”

A coffee cart or pop-up one block from Moscone, away from the show floor. Anyone who solves a 3-minute challenge, with harder flags unlocked on their phone all week, receives free coffee, and a live leaderboard ranks companies against each other. Every company on the board becomes a lead, the leaderboard gives attendees something to post about, and the format puts SkillBit's core idea (prove the skill) in front of people who walk up to it.

  1. Walk up to the cart
  2. Capture a 3-minute flag
  3. Company joins the live leaderboard
  4. Results email and a link to book time

RSAC rules and city permits to be confirmed first.

Challenge design

Three formats, one leaderboard.

  1. Option 1

    60-second speed round

    Phone-friendly
    • Scan a QR code for five rapid-fire items. Real or malicious? (a phishing email, a login alert, a suspicious link)
    • Scored on accuracy and speed
    • Anyone can play while standing in line for coffee
  2. Option 2

    3-minute single flag

    Recommended lead
    • One scenario, one answer. For example, “Here are 10 log lines. Which IP address is the attacker?”
    • Short enough to finish at the cart, but still feels like real security work
  3. Option 3

    Flag ladder

    Runs all week
    • One easy flag at the cart (2 to 3 minutes)
    • Two harder flags unlock on the phone afterward
    • The leaderboard stays open all week, so people keep playing from their hotel or between sessions without holding up the line

How the formats combine

  1. At the cart

    The 3-minute single flag

  2. QR code on cups and stickers

    The flag ladder, so the challenge travels around the conference without a full-time staffer

  3. Leaderboard

    Fastest correct time by company, closing Thursday afternoon, with winners announced on LinkedIn

  4. Follow-up line

    “That took you three minutes. Here’s what 20 minutes with your whole team would show you.”The 20-minute rule stays the product pitch, and the event format stays short.

Programs

AI does the research and follow-up. Partners stretch the budget.

AI-enabled programs

  • Target list from public signals

    The signal-listening workflow behind about 30% of new leads at Implicit can pull the RSAC speaker and sponsor lists and LinkedIn posts such as “headed to RSAC.” Claude scores the accounts and drafts outreach that references each prospect's talk or post.

  • Personalized challenge invitations

    Claude reads a prospect's open job postings and selects the matching Labs challenge, so the invitation reads “here is the challenge built for your cloud security candidates, try it at RSAC.”

  • Instant results email

    Every street challenge participant receives a short skills snapshot within the hour, with a link to book time.

  • Same-day meeting follow-up

    Notes from each meeting become a draft follow-up that the account executive approves the same night.

Capital-efficient programs

  • Co-hosted dinners

    A non-competing vendor that sells to the same buyer (identity or SOC tooling, for example) splits the cost and the guest list. That turns two dinners into four, or frees up $5K.

  • Customer-hosted dinners

    A security leader from a customer co-hosting a dinner fills seats better than a vendor invitation.

  • Community events

    A sponsored challenge at BSides SF or a local ISSA meetup that week replaces a standalone event.

  • Published findings

    One week after the show, anonymized leaderboard results are published (“What 300 practitioners showed in 3 minutes”). The report creates a second round of follow-up and a PR story from data already collected.

Success criteria

Measured before, during and after the event.

  1. Before the event

    Meetings booked against a target of 25 by April 1, reviewed weekly

  2. At the event

    Meetings held and show rate

  3. After the event

    Opportunities created within 45 days, pipeline dollars, cost per opportunity

All activity is tagged “RSAC27” in the CRM from the first email.

Featured initiative

A free “job description to skills test” tool

A security leader pastes in a job description, and Claude returns a 20-minute assessment plan for that role built from SkillBit's library. Every user of the tool displays the exact signal this program targets, so outbound starts generating inbound. The tool is inexpensive to build as a simple AI-powered page, and its timing lines up with the hiring assessment launch.

  1. Paste a job description
  2. Receive a 20-minute assessment plan
  3. The visit registers as a buying signal

Programs

AI handles research and first drafts, so BDR time goes to conversations.

AI-enabled programs

  • Posting analysis

    Claude extracts the skills each posting requires (cloud, incident response, AppSec) and maps them to SkillBit labs, so outreach can reference what the company is hiring for.

  • First-draft outreach

    Claude drafts the email from the posting, and the BDR edits and sends it. Research time drops and 50 accounts per week stays realistic.

  • Automated weekly scorecard

    Every Monday a Slack post compares results to plan, so no one builds a report by hand.

  • Reply classification

    AI sorts reply text by objection (“already hired,” “no budget,” “wrong person”) so the messaging improves every week.

Capital-efficient programs

  • Labs user job changes

    A Labs user who joins a new company is a warm contact, often on a growing team.

  • Hiring posts on LinkedIn

    Security leaders post open roles on LinkedIn, sometimes before the job boards, and the listening workflow catches them quickly.

  • Security recruiter partnerships

    Staffing agencies see hiring before roles are posted, and a referral fee or co-marketing arrangement costs very little.

  • Low-friction offer

    “Free assessments for your first five candidates on this role” is an easier yes than a demo.

  • Personalized video for Tier A

    A 60-second screen recording of the account's job posting beside the matching challenge takes a BDR about five minutes.
