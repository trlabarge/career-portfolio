Initiative 1

The post-acute referral benchmark

Publish a real dataset on how referrals move through skilled nursing facilities.

What one report produces

The opportunity

Every vendor in this category makes the same claims in the same language. Buyers have no independent reference point for what good performance looks like, and no vendor claim can be verified.

Magicare's platform holds data no competitor has.

  • Referral response times
  • Referral-to-admission conversion
  • Time to decision
  • Authorization turnaround
  • Census patterns by operator size and region

Published as an industry benchmark, Magicare's data becomes a reference point for the industry. The company that publishes that benchmark becomes the authority.

Why it could reach buyers earlier than other channels

Operators who are already looking

ConferencesReferrals

Operators who do not yet know they have a problem

The benchmark

An owner who learns the median referral sits unanswered for a given number of hours, and then realizes no one at their organization knows that number, has entered the market without a vendor conversation ever taking place. That makes Magicare's benchmark a mechanism that creates new demand.

It closes the trade publication gap

Trade press often runs original industry data. An original, data-driven benchmark is an asset that can earn that coverage, and benchmark data keeps getting cited for years after publication.

The core asset

  • 1 reportProperly designed
  • ~15 pagesRoughly, in length
  • 3 or 4Findings that are genuinely surprising
  • 1 headlineA number that makes an operator uncomfortable about their own performance

Where AI carries the load

  • Pattern analysis

    Across the exported dataset, compressing an analyst engagement into days.

  • Narrative and derivatives

    The narrative and every derivative asset, drafted from one source of truth.

  • Interactive SNF grader

    Built directly in code, so engineering time stays on the product.

  • Trade outreach

    Research on the right editor at each outlet, and a pitch drafted for each one.

  • Segment variants

    The same findings, re-cut for each audience. A large multi-facility operator and an RCM vendor each get a version built around the numbers that apply to them.

Requirements

  • Platform data access, with legal and customer consent review
  • Agreed anonymization thresholds
  • Named customers alongside the findings

Open question

Is customer volume sufficient for statistically defensible numbers?

The opportunity

Competitive evaluations in this category collapse into feature count and price, because buyers have no framework for comparing products that all describe themselves the same way. Those two dimensions favor whoever has the most funding.

A published evaluation framework changes what buyers measure, before any vendor conversation begins.

Why this works upstream of the deal

  1. Before the deal

    An operator who structures an evaluation around published criteria has had the competition shaped before the first vendor call.

  2. During the deal

    The framework can improve close rates in deals already in motion, so it returns faster than most category work.

  3. In sales hands

    Sales gets an asset that reframes an active evaluation and reads as guidance to the buyer.

The core asset

Create a public buyer's evaluation guide with six to eight criteria. Criteria should include a question each prospect can ask themselves, guidance on what a strong answer looks like to those questions, and how to verify the answer independently.

The guide must include at least one criterion where Magicare holds no obvious advantage. Without it, the guide reads as marketing and gets discarded.

    What it produces

    • The public guide
    • A scored self-assessment operators can complete
    • A sales edition for active evaluations
    • One content piece per criterion, six to eight pieces from a single effort
    • A panel or webinar with an operator and an independent consultant
    • Thought leadership, leading to potential trade publication articles or conference sessions
    • Messaging and positioning foundation for subsequent marketing material

    Where AI carries the load

    • Interview synthesis

      Extracts the language buyers use and the objections that recur. This is weeks of manual work and the stage where most positioning efforts stall.

    • Competitive analysis

      Maps what every competitor claims across vendor sites, materials, and public reviews, and identifies unclaimed ground.

    • Drafting

      The criteria set and every derivative, drafted from one agreed document.

    • A version for each buyer

      Owners and operations leaders judge these products on different terms. Each gets a version of the guide written around the questions they ask.

    • Regeneration

      When a competitor shifts positioning, the affected pieces can be quickly adjusted and regenerated.

    Requirements

    • Working sessions with leadership and sales on genuine differentiation
    • Customer access for interviews
    • Willingness to publish criteria where Magicare is not strongest, which is what makes the framework credible

    Open question

    Is the differentiation structural or executional?

    If it is executional, the framework is the wrong instrument and a different approach applies.

    Initiative 3

    The market intelligence engine

    A continuous loop where real buyer conversations determine the content that gets created next.

    Listening and content engine loop

    1 Listen of 6, continuous

    1. Six sources, monitored continuously.

      • LinkedIn comment threads, where this market demonstrably engages
      • Trade publication articles and the discussion around them
      • State and national association groups
      • Discharge planners, case managers, and RCM vendor staff
      • Review sites where evaluation research happens
      • Conference agendas, a published signal of the topics the industry considers important

    2. Each captured conversation is rated on three tests.

      • Buying-group membership
      • Fit against the large-operator profile
      • Topical relevance

    3. Three primary listening and content streams, each with defined downstream actions.

      • Pain pointsContent and messaging
      • Competitor discussionThe evaluation framework
      • Direct mentionsResponse and customer proof

    4. Leadership and sales respond in their own voices with substantive answers. AI drafts replies in each person's voice with the context summarized, which cuts the workflow to a few minutes. Nothing publishes without human approval.

      • Recurring pain pointsThe next core assets
      • Buyer languageSite and campaign language
      • Competitor objectionsEvaluation criteria
      • Repeated questionsTool features
      • Where conversations happenWhere content is distributed
      • Engagement by topic
      • Named accounts showing more engagement
      • Conversations converting to meetings

      Results tune the scoring model and the cycle repeats.

    The opportunity

    Conventional content planning sets a calendar in advance and measures as it is published to determine its effectiveness. A listening-led system shortens that loop and grounds every asset in language buyers are using.

    Every piece of content starts from what buyers are saying right now. That is how one person keeps pace with a larger competitor team.

    Listening never pauses. Content ships all month, each piece drawing on the most recent core asset.

    Two ways to organize the work

    Where AI carries the load

    • Continuous monitoring

      Software checks LinkedIn, trade press, association groups, and review sites every day and flags new conversations worth a look. Doing the same by hand would be a full-time job.

    • Classification at volume

      Every conversation checked against the named-account list.

    • Trend detection

      Tracks how often each topic comes up from month to month. When one complaint starts appearing far more often, the change shows up in the counts, even when no single post makes it obvious.

    • Voice-matched drafting

      Drafts in each person's voice, and every draft goes to that person for approval.

    • A ranked production queue

      Dozens of conversations synthesized into a ranked list of what to make next, with the buyers' own phrasing attached.

    Requirements

    • Leadership participation and review
    • An agreed voice and approval workflow
    • A modest tooling budget

    Open question

    Is online conversation volume in this market sufficient to sustain the listening input?

    If it is thin, recorded customer calls and conference floor conversations supplement the ideation engine, ensuring it still runs on buyer signal and language.
