Three ideas to make some noise

For Daniel Schoenbrun and the Magicare team

You asked for two. Here are three, which is partly the point. The question you raised was how one marketer competes with a five-person team at a better-funded competitor. The honest answer is not by working harder. It is by picking fewer, bigger pieces of work and then exhausting every format each one supports, with AI doing the production lifting that would otherwise require headcount.

These three are built that way, and they are built to feed each other. I am proposing them from outside, without having seen your data, your margins, or your win-loss record, so treat each one as a starting hypothesis rather than a plan.

01

Idea 1

The post-acute referral benchmark

Publish the first real dataset on how referrals actually move through skilled nursing facilities, using your own anonymized platform data.

Read the detailClose the detail

What it is

Response times, referral-to-admission conversion, time to decision, authorization turnaround, census patterns cut by operator size and region. One properly designed report, roughly 15 pages, with three or four findings that are genuinely surprising and a headline number that makes an operator uncomfortable about their own performance.

Why it works here

You said the product is market-leading but the claim is hard to convey amid the noise around AI. Every competitor is making the same assertions with the same words. Data is the one claim none of them can assert their way past, because it either exists or it does not.

You also named trade publications as a gap. Original industry data is what trade press runs. Vendor announcements get ignored. Benchmarks get written up, cited, and referenced for years.

Why it creates demand, not just attention

It reaches buyers before they know they have a problem. An owner who reads that the median referral sits unanswered for some number of hours, and realizes they have no idea what their own number is, has entered the funnel without anyone selling to them. Conferences and referrals cannot do that, because both only reach people already in motion.

What one piece becomes

  • The report
  • A press push to the long-term care trades
  • A webinar walking the findings, with a customer on it
  • A conference session proposal, which is how you earn speaking slots instead of renting booths
  • An interactive grader where an operator enters their own numbers and sees where they rank
  • 20 to 30 social posts across you, Chen, Bob and Sam, each built on a single finding
  • A sales one-pager per finding
  • Segment cuts, so the 20+ facility operator version differs from the RCM vendor version
  • An annual rhythm, so year two is a trend story and the benchmark becomes a fixture

Where AI does the lifting

  • Analysis across the exported dataset to find and test patterns, compressing what would be an analyst engagement into days
  • Drafting the narrative and every derivative asset from one source of truth
  • Building the grader tool directly with Claude Code rather than queuing engineering time
  • Researching the right editors at each trade outlet and drafting tailored pitches
  • Generating the segment variants, which is normally the first work cut for lack of hours

What it would need from you

Platform data access and whatever legal or customer consent review that requires. A decision on anonymization thresholds. Ideally a customer or two willing to be named alongside it.

The open question

This needs enough customer volume for the numbers to hold up. I cannot see that from outside, so it is the first thing I would check.

02

Idea 2

Name the category and publish the buying scorecard

Define what this category actually is, then publish the criteria operators should use to evaluate any vendor in it, including you.

Read the detailClose the detail

What it is

A public buyer's evaluation guide. Six to eight criteria, each with what to ask, what a good answer looks like, and how to verify it rather than take the vendor's word. It has to include at least one criterion where Magicare does not obviously win, or it reads as marketing and gets dismissed.

Why it works here

You said the product is market-leading but hard to distinguish. Sam said bake-offs collapse into feature and price comparison. Those are the same problem from two seats.

Buyers have no framework for comparing these products, so they fall back on the two dimensions anyone can understand, which are feature count and price. Both favor whoever has more money. Supplying a better framework is how you compete without outspending.

Why it creates demand, not just attention

It works upstream of the deal. If an operator uses your criteria to structure their evaluation, the competition is shaped before the vendor calls start.

It also improves win rate in deals already in flight, which means it pays back faster than most brand work, and it gives sales something to send mid-cycle that reframes an active bake-off without sounding like a pitch.

What one piece becomes

  • The public guide
  • A scored self-assessment an operator can complete
  • A sales version for mid-cycle deals
  • One content piece per criterion, so six to eight pieces from one effort
  • A conference talk, since this is a session topic rather than a pitch
  • A webinar or panel with an operator and ideally a consultant or analyst
  • Trade bylines, which addresses the publication gap directly
  • The messaging spine for the site

Where AI does the lifting

  • Synthesizing recorded customer interviews to extract the language buyers actually use, the recurring objections, and what made them switch. Normally weeks of manual work, and where most positioning exercises quietly die
  • Systematic competitive analysis across competitor sites, materials and public reviews to map what everyone claims and find the unclaimed ground
  • Drafting the criteria set and every derivative from one agreed document
  • Generating variants for each buyer layer, which matters because you described an owner removed from operations and people below who know the detail
  • Keeping it current, so when a competitor shifts positioning the affected pieces regenerate in hours

What it would need from you

Time with you, Sam, Chen and Bob on what genuinely differentiates Magicare beyond Epic compatibility. Customer access for interviews. Willingness to include criteria where you are not strongest, which is the thing that makes it credible.

The open question

I do not yet know what your real differentiators are. The first step is finding out, and if the honest answer is that you win on execution rather than anything structural, the scorecard is the wrong play and I would say so rather than build it anyway.

03

Idea 3

The market intelligence engine

A continuous loop where what buyers are actually saying determines what gets made next, instead of a content calendar set in a planning meeting and found to be wrong a quarter later.

Read the detailClose the detail

What it is

Six stages, running continuously.

  1. Listen

    LinkedIn comment threads, where this market demonstrably engages. Trade publication articles and the discussion around them. State and national association groups. Adjacent audiences who talk more publicly than owners do, specifically discharge planners, case managers and RCM vendor staff. Review sites where bake-off research happens. Conference agendas, which are a published signal of what the industry decided matters this year.

  2. Score

    Every conversation rated on whether the person sits in the buying group, whether the organization matches the 20+ facility profile, and whether the topic is one Magicare can speak to credibly. Only what clears the bar reaches a human.

  3. Categorize

    Three buckets with different downstream actions. Pain points feed content and messaging. Competitor mentions feed the scorecard criteria. Direct Magicare mentions feed response and customer proof.

  4. Engage

    You, Chen, Bob and Sam respond in your own voices, with useful answers and Magicare mentioned only where relevant. AI drafts in each person's voice with context summarized, so review takes two minutes instead of twenty. Nothing publishes without a human reading it.

  5. Feed the calendar

    Recurring pain points become the next core pieces. The exact language buyers use becomes the language on the site. Objections in competitor threads become scorecard criteria. Repeated questions become tool features. Where the conversations happen determines where content gets distributed.

  6. Measure and restart

    Which topics drove engagement, which named accounts went from silent to engaged, which conversations became meetings. That tunes the scoring and the loop runs again.

Then back to stage 1, with sharper scoring.

Why it works here

You said you need high-volume content creation and asked how one person competes with a five-person team. This is the structural answer.

A larger team organized by channel produces more ordinary assets, planned in advance and measured in arrears. One person running this loop produces fewer core assets, but each one is grounded in something a real buyer said that month. It wins on relevance and speed rather than headcount.

The production math

  • 1core research piece a month
  • 20 to 30derivative assets from it
  • Dailyleadership posts across four accounts
  • 1trade byline a month

That is a concrete answer to high-volume, and I would want to know whether it matches what you had in mind.

Where AI does the lifting

  • Continuous monitoring across many surfaces, which is a full-time role for a person and a background process otherwise
  • Classification at volume against the named-account list
  • Trend detection over time, which is where it genuinely beats a human, because noticing that authorization complaints tripled this quarter is invisible when you read threads one at a time
  • Voice-matched drafting with human approval
  • Synthesizing hundreds of conversations into a ranked list of what to make next, with the actual phrasing attached

What it would need from you

Leadership willingness to post and review. Agreement on voice and approval workflow. A small tooling budget.

The open question

Sam made the point that your buyers are not in open online communities the way other markets are, and that deserves to be taken seriously. So the first two weeks are about confirming where the signal actually is. If the online surface turns out thin, recorded customer calls and conference floor conversations feed the identical engine through a different door. The input is what matters, not the channel.
