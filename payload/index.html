Payload 12-Month Marketing Plan

From a strong real estate foundation to a repeatable growth engine

A phased plan to turn Payload's customers, data, and reach into predictable pipeline in real estate, and then expands into adjacent industries with similar needs.

AToday

A leader in earnest money payments with 3,000+ real estate customers, 20+ software integrations, and banking partnerships with J.P. Morgan and Fifth Third.

BIn 12 months

A marketing engine that produces steady pipeline, grows revenue from existing customers, and has a proven path into more industries.

The ambition

Payload becomes the name people think of when they want to move money in industries that still run on checks and wires.

Real estate is the foundation. Prove out the playbook, scale the motions that work, and then expand into other industries.

How this plan grows Payload

Six levers work together across 4 phases to facilitate growth.

  1. 1

    Grow through software partners

    Payload's 20+ integrations are the primary method for awareness and distribution. Joint marketing with top partners and targeted campaigns to a list of ~3,000 platforms turn that reach into more users and new integrations.

    • Partners, Associations, and Events
  2. 2

    Turn interest into sales conversations

    Listening tools find potential buyers talking about wire fraud, slow payments, and other pain points. Leaders reach out personally, target accounts get extra attention, and good fits go to sales. Automation handles the searching and sequencing, so leaders spend their time engaging directly.

    • Listening and Outreach
    • Paid Media
  3. 3

    Turn leadership voices and Payload's data into reach

    Leaders post every week, including a potential technical voice for developers. A regular Payments Index report turns Payload's payment and fraud data into news, posts, and sales talking points.

    • Thought Leadership and PR
  4. 4

    Get more people using Payload

    When a brokerage signs, not every agent starts using Payload. Most customers also start with earnest money. Moving them into commissions, rent, agent fees, and vendor payments is a smooth transition into new revenue.

    • Lifecycle and Adoption
  5. 5

    Give developers a front door

    Payload wins deals on its tech stack and APIs, but developers often pick Stripe because they can sign up on their own. Clear docs, sample code, and a fast sandbox make Payload easy to try.

    • Product and Developer Marketing
    • Website and Measurement
  6. 6

    Scale into more industries

    Payload is already winning customers outside real estate. Once the expanded real estate GTM playbook runs smoothly, it rolls into the industries showing traction. A formal go or no-go review decides where to put more budget.

    • New Industries

Team and capacity

Built for a lean team of two marketers, AI tools, and part-time specialists.

  • Marketer 1

    Strategy, CEO thought leadership, partner marketing, target accounts, paid media, the listening and outreach engine, and reporting.

  • Marketer 2

    Content engine, product and developer marketing, lifecycle and adoption, and customer stories.

  • AI tools

    First drafts, repurposing, research, lead scoring, and personalization, using tools like Claude, PhantomBuster, Make, and lemlist or Dripify.

  • Contractors

    Design and video, paid media setup, and a technical writer for developer content, as needed.

  • Leadership time. Leaders own partner relationships and are the main voices, so marketing does the heavy lifting behind them.

  • Sales capacity. Lead volume matched to what sales can work.

  • Compliance. Payments content gets reviewed before it goes out.

  • Focus. A two-person team can go deep on one or two new industries at a time.

Capacity rule. No more than 3 or 4 new programs launching at once.

How the engine works

  1. Ideas. Payload's data, its customers, and its leaders create the ideas.
  2. Content. AI turns each idea into dozens of assets.
  3. Distribution. Leaders, partners, customers, press, and paid spread them.
  4. Signals. Listening and tracking show who engages.
  5. Pipeline. Outreach, target account programs, partners, and sales start conversations.
  6. Growth. Adoption and lifecycle programs grow each customer, and their stories restart the cycle.
01 Ideas Payload's data, its customers, and its leaders create the ideas.

Measurement

Pipeline and revenue

  • Pipeline and revenue by industry, channel, partner, and program
  • Cost per opportunity by channel
  • Meetings from outreach and target accounts, by sender
  • Partner-sourced pipeline and new integrations signed

Adoption and growth

  • Time to first payment for new customers
  • Payment types and volume per customer
  • Share of agents active at enterprise accounts
  • Share of partners' users who are active

Reach and reputation

  • Reach and engagement for C-suite and other leaders
  • Trade press coverage
  • Presence in AI search answers
  • Developer engagement, like docs and GitHub traffic and sandbox requests

What we'd learn in the first 30 days

  1. Whether product usage data, including agent and partner-user adoption, reaches the CRM
  2. How many customers use more than one payment type
  3. Which industries respond most to the 3,000-platform campaign
  4. Whether a self-serve sandbox is on the product roadmap
  5. The budget for paid media and contractors, and how many leads sales can handle
  6. What review public content about payments needs
  7. How much time C-suite leaders can give each week
  8. What gets engagement on leadership posts, and who is engaging
