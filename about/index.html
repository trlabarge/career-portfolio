The best marketing doesn’t feel like marketing.

Why marketing

Building things that didn’t exist yet.

I’ve spent my career building things that didn’t exist yet, then figuring out how to get people to care. Turns out that’s the whole job.

Photo coming soon

Made from nothing. I got into marketing because I liked making things out of nothing.

Then pipeline showed up. Somewhere in the last 15 years, a second personality showed up, one that cares about pipeline, ARR, and whether a campaign actually worked. The creative kid never left. He just got a day job in revenue.

The timeshare problem. Most marketing feels like being cornered at a party by someone selling a timeshare.

The good stuff disappears. The good stuff doesn’t feel like marketing at all. It’s just useful, at the right moment, for the right person, often before they’ve fully realized they needed it. That’s the whole job, really. Everything else is decoration.

How I lead

Player coach, literally.

I’m a player coach, and I mean that literally.

Close to the work. Staying close to the actual work means I know what’s working, what’s quietly on fire, and what we’re paying for that nobody uses.

Hire well, then get out of the way. I hire people I trust and then leave them alone, mostly. I’ll teach, unblock, and occasionally rescue, but I don’t hover. Hovering usually means you hired the wrong person, or don’t trust the right one.

Pick the right tool. The most useful skill right now, AI or no AI, is knowing what’s worth building and picking the right tool for the job. I’ve leaned on that to get a lot done with small teams, which is either efficient or a symptom of never having a big enough budget. Possibly both.

Harmony over silos. I also happen to like harmony, which sounds soft until you’ve worked somewhere without it. Marketing operating in its own silo, disconnected from sales, product, and customer success, is a special kind of chaos. I’d rather we all just talk to each other.

What’s next

Still figuring things out, in real time.
